# Art Direction

Source: directive §3, §6, §7, §8, §13 (visual identity of temporal tech).

## Identity
Grounded sci-fi realism + advanced but *engineered* technology + cinematic architecture + industrial detail + temporal distortion.
Every environment, character, prop and effect belongs to one universe. Every design decision must look intentional.
Avoid: random sci-fi greebles, gratuitous neon, generic futuristic shapes, inconsistent assets.

## Design languages
- **HELIX (corporate/engineered):** clean industrial design, structural logic (loads, supports, service routes), modular panel systems, safety
  colours (orange = HELIX identity, cool white work-lights, amber emergency), stencilled labels, rated equipment, cable trays, vents that lead somewhere.
- **Chronal technology:** one consistent motif: cyan-white energy that reads as *time*: layered ripples, banding that flows, afterimages, fragmenting
  light, reversed particle motion. Used sparingly so it stays meaningful. Interfaces use thin geometric linework, never random glow.
- **Fractured timelines:** each timeline has its own palette and light: future = cool, clean, high-key glass/steel; present/ruined = desaturated,
  warm emergency light, dust, damage; past = warmer, analogue materials. Landmarks persist across states.
- **People:** believable construction: clothing with seams, folds, thickness; armour following the body; functional equipment; distinct silhouettes
  per character (Ethan: dark practical operator kit; Echo: inverted/desaturated mirror with fractured chronal glow; Imani: lab/field layers, copper-warm
  accents; Voss: immaculate executive tailoring turning chronal-integrated; Nyx: lean temporal-security armour with experimental modules).

## Environments (per chapter identity)
1. HELIX Research Facility: underground complex, experimental chambers, scientific machinery, terminals, industrial infrastructure, emergency lighting,
   damage, anomalies, destruction. **The opening must demonstrate the game's graphics quality immediately.**
2. Fractured City: skyscrapers, streets, sidewalks, infrastructure, vehicles, storefronts, rooftops, interiors, weathering, alternate-timeline structures. No empty streets, no repeated buildings.
3. Corporate Arcology: executive offices, grand spaces, labs, security, glass + metal, detailed interiors, temporal research installations.
4. Multiple Timelines: pristine future / abandoned / earlier historical / transforming; recognisable landmarks across states.
5. Temporal Nexus: monumental architecture, floating structures, fractured reality, large-scale environmental animation, a cinematic final arena. The most spectacular space.

## Environment rules
Believable architecture (beams, columns, panels, door frames, windows, ceiling structure, fixtures, ventilation, pipes, cables, floors with variation,
furniture, machinery, decals, interactive equipment, damage, debris). Modular kits without visible repetition. Geometry for structure, textures/normal maps for fine detail.
Spaces communicate purpose (labs look like labs, offices have real equipment). Worlds feel inhabited and functional. Never empty rooms with basic walls.

## Colour and grading
Purposeful colour temperature per space; strong contrast and composition; atmospheric depth; avoid flat uniform lighting and excessively dark scenes.
Post grading must support the palette, not rescue weak assets.
