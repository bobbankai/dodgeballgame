# INKBOUND

A short, story-driven third-person adventure set inside a sketchbook.

## Story

**Prologue — The Attic.** A rainy night. Ada, 12, climbs into the attic studio of her
mother, an illustrator who died last spring. On the desk lies the sketchbook her mother
never finished, its cover inscribed *"For Ada — when you're ready."* Every page is
drawn except the last, which holds only a pencilled door. Ada touches it and is pulled in.

**Chapter 1 — The Margin Village.** A gentle village of crooked cottages and a windmill,
drawn in graphite. Colour only lives where her mother finished painting. Ada meets **Pip**,
a fox folded from a torn page, sharp-tongued and kind, who knows the book better than
anyone. Places her mother never finished are faint pencil outlines; Ada's pencil can
**ink them in**: trace a bridge and it becomes real. Something is rubbing the world out:
**the Smudge**, a creeping grey haze that erases whatever it touches. Ada restores the
village's **green** pigment and watches the meadow flood with colour.

**Chapter 2 — The Folded Sea.** A sea of drawn wave-lines and a lonely lighthouse kept by
an old paper crane. Ada inks a boat and crosses. Margin notes in her mother's handwriting
appear along the way: she kept putting off the last page, because finishing the book
meant saying goodbye. A storm: the Smudge swallows the horizon and Ada barely outruns it.
She restores the **blue**.

**Chapter 3 — The Unfinished Page.** Blank paper in every direction, the Smudge's
country. There is nothing to trace, so Ada has to draw her own path. Pip admits he was
drawn on the back of the last page: her mother always drew herself as a fox. The Smudge
is not a monster. It is every eraser mark in the book, the urge to undo, grief. Ada
doesn't fight it. She draws *with* it, turning the smudges into shading, because every
drawing needs its shadows. With the **gold** restored she finishes the last page herself:
her mother and her, side by side.

**Epilogue.** Morning in the attic. The rain has stopped. Ada opens a new sketchbook.

## Play

- **Explore** small hand-built spaces in third person; jump, climb low ledges, talk.
- **Ink-in**: walk to a pencil outline and hold *E* to trace it. The line darkens as Ada
  draws, then colour washes in and the object becomes solid (bridges, stairs, a boat, keys).
- **Pigments**: each chapter's goal; restoring one floods the area with colour in a
  watercolour bloom from where Ada stands.
- **Margin notes**: optional handwritten notes from her mother; the emotional backbone.
- **The Smudge**: chase sequences and erased areas that must be redrawn.
- No combat. Failure is gentle: a rubbed-out Ada reappears at the last inked checkpoint.

## Look: ink and watercolour

- Graphite world that turns to watercolour as pigments return (a world-space colour mask
  with a bleeding watercolour edge).
- Thin ink lines on silhouettes and creases that *boil* slightly, like hand-drawn
  animation frames; heavier lines up close, lighter with distance.
- Flat washes with one soft shadow tone, cross-hatching in deep shadow, paper grain and
  pigment granulation over everything; distance fades to the paper colour.
- Characters and props modelled and animated in Blender; shapes simple and confident,
  with a slightly hand-made wobble — nothing ruler-straight.

## Characters

- **Ada**: 12, yellow raincoat, red scarf, rubber boots, satchel, a pencil behind her ear.
- **Pip**: an origami fox, white paper with her mother's ink notes on his back.
- **The Smudge**: graphite haze with eraser crumbs; never fully seen.
- **The Lighthouse Keeper**: an old paper crane who talks in tide tables.
